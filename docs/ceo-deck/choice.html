Three ways to make the data fit for AI

Option A

Validate by hand

Check the 740,000 untrustworthy pages: about 23 person-years. Every page, for AI-ready data: about 113. New filings add 0.6–1.5 people a year.

Assumes 3 minutes a page and 1,600 working hours a year.

Option B

Automate, manage the risk

Machines read everything: about $20,000 of cloud extraction, or days of a local GPU. Errors are flagged, not prevented, and no hand-checked ground truth exists yet.

At $5.53 per 1,000 pages, measured on 90,484 pages in 2026.

Option C

Digital first, at the source

Most of the 23% is a setting or a habit: export, don’t scan; sign digitally; file data as data. A check at upload names the failing page while the source still exists.

Errors prevented, not found.

Recommended: C for every new filing; B for the existing record, with A only on the pages B flags.

Page counts from the census; cloud cost from the cer-regdocs2 evaluation (Azure, August 2026). Person-years are an estimate from the stated assumptions.

Option A: people check the data. At an assumed three minutes to check a page against its image, the 740,000 pages without trustworthy text are about 23 person-years; checking every page so all data is AI-ready is over 100. New filings add less than two people a year - the backlog is the problem. Option B: let machines read everything and manage the risk. It is cheap - about twenty thousand dollars at a cloud service's rate, measured on 90,000 pages - but errors are only flagged, and we still have no hand-checked ground truth. Option C: fix it at the source. Most of the problem is how files are produced. A rule plus an automatic check at upload prevents the errors rather than finding them later. The recommendation is to combine them: digital first for everything new, automation for the existing record, and people only where the machine flags doubt.
