Evidence that can prove it was not altered

Today

The legal signature is a paper receipt, mailed within three business days. REGDOCS shows no fingerprint (hash) of any file, so no one can prove a copy is the one filed.

Only 1,410 of 269,025 documents (0.5%) carry a digital signature, and 105 of those had something saved after signing.

Proposed

  • A SHA-256 fingerprint of every file at receipt, published on the record
  • PAdES digital signatures, with long-term validation
  • A trusted timestamp in place of the paper receipt
  • A signed filing manifest: every file, its fingerprint, and the data files it supports

Tamper-evident and machine-readable in the same step: the manifest is the chain of custody and the table of contents for automation.

Current rules: Filer’s Guide to Electronic Submission (June 2025). Signature counts: integrity pass over every PDF (October 2026).

Automated decisions need evidence whose integrity can be shown later without paper. Today the signature of record is a paper receipt, and nothing on REGDOCS lets anyone prove a downloaded file is the one filed; we found the same file filed under three IDs. The proposal: fingerprint every file at receipt and publish the fingerprint, accept digital signatures with long-term validation, timestamp the receipt, and have every filing carry a signed manifest. The manifest makes a filing tamper-evident and tells automation exactly what arrived, in one step.
